## 2. Response to the Agenda's "Cool Question": Proposed Speaking Topics

### Background & Strategic Framing
The departure from a traditional academic format creates an extraordinary opening. Academic panels often risk distancing international visitors and neighborhood residents with abstract theory. By contrasting **Playground One** (ground-level neighborhood creators and machizukuri builders) with **Playground Two** (metabolic sociology, spatial memory, and adaptive housing), we create a warm, conversational, and deeply engaging forum.

---

### Playground One: Grassroots Machizukuri & Cultural Stewardship
*Curated in partnership with Goto-san, bringing together Kyojima artists, shopkeepers, and renovation craftspeople.*

#### Topic Option 1A (Recommended): 
**"The Living Nagaya: Craft, Art, and Autonomous Placemaking in Kyojima"**  
*(『長屋の生きた更新：京島における工芸・芸術・自律的なまちづくり』)*
- **Core Premise**: Tokyo’s historical wooden nagaya districts are frequently characterized as vulnerable or outdated by master planners. In Kyojima, local artists, carpenters, and residents have proven the opposite: through gentle, self-organized renovations, open storefronts, and shared alleyways, the neighborhood acts as an open, collective living room.
- **Key Discussion Inquiries**:
  1. How do artists integrate into an existing multi-generational neighborhood without displacing long-time residents?
  2. How can informal, un-zoned spaces (alley gardens, open workshops, bench thresholds) foster daily social trust?
  3. What are the practical mechanics of restoring century-old timber structures on tight budgets using traditional wood joinery and salvaged materials?
- **Panel Format**: Three 10-minute visual storytelling vignettes by local makers, followed by an open 35-minute fishbowl dialogue with audience participation.

#### Topic Option 1B:
**"Alleys as Living Rooms: The Social Fabric of Shitamachi Micropublics"**  
*(『路地はみんなの居間：下町における小さな公共空間の生態系』)*
- **Core Premise**: Focusing on the micro-geography of Kyojima—potted plant clusters, communal rainwater pumps (*Rojison*), laundry lines, and engawa benches—as functional shared infrastructure that builds disaster resilience and human warmth.

---

### Playground Two: Metabolic Sociology & Living Architecture
*Centered around ethnographic researcher Yuka upon confirmation, or framed around community-led architectural memory.*

#### Topic Option 2A (With Yuka):
**"Metabolic Sociology: Living Communities as Adaptive Organisms"**  
*(『メタボリック・ソシオロジー：適応する生態系としての生活空間』)*
- **Core Premise**: The 1960s Japanese Metabolist architectural movement envisioned mega-structures with replaceable capsule pods. In reality, the true Japanese metabolism is found right here in the shitamachi nagaya: organic, incremental timber repairs, seasonal plant migrations, and adaptive domestic routines that evolve over decades.
- **Key Discussion Inquiries**:
  1. What can contemporary sociology learn from the daily routines and mutual assistance networks of elderly nagaya residents?
  2. How does "social metabolism" operate when new creative migrants arrive in historic working-class quarters?
  3. How can digital archiving honor living residents rather than turning their neighborhood into a static outdoor museum?
- **Panel Format**: A 20-minute visual keynote by Yuka outlining her field research in Kyojima, followed by a responsive dialogue with Goto-san and visiting urban practitioners.

#### Topic Option 2B (If Yuka is Unavailable):
**"Memory and Materiality: Documenting Neighborhoods Without Displacing Them"**  
*(『記憶と物質性：失われない街の記録と尊厳』)*
- **Core Premise**: How oral historians, independent photographers, and 3D digital archivists collaborate with local communities to document fragile architectural heritage before natural decay or redevelopment occurs.

---

### Cross-District Synthesis Panel: Kyojima × Koganecho
*Connecting our host district in Sumida, Tokyo with our partner community in Koganecho, Yokohama.*

#### Topic Option 3:
**"Two Decades of Bottom-Up Culture: From Railway Viaducts to Living Alleys"**  
*(『高架下から路地へ：自律的文化発信の20年』)*
- **Core Premise**: Koganecho reclaimed its post-industrial waterfront and railway arches through artist residencies and community art management. Kyojima preserves its alleyways through architectural reuse and cultural grassroots activism. What lessons can these two iconic community movements share?
- **Speakers**: Goto-san (Kyojima) in conversation with Liu Shujia & Ryo Suzuki (Koganecho Area Management Center).
